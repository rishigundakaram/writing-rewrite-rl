## plainness_and_decluttering
Use the fewest, plainest words that say exactly what you mean. Cut puffery, redundancy, euphemism, hedges, and needless scaffolding so the reader sees who does what, fast.
- inflated_diction_plain_alternative_exists: Use of a long Latinate word or padded phrase where a short, plain word would convey the same meaning in general nonfiction. Doesn’t apply to technical terms used in their proper domain, proper names, or cases where the longer form adds a needed nuance. Example violation: at this point in time. NOT a violation: Precipitation will exceed one inch along the coast.
- euphemistic_bureaucratic_camouflage: Euphemisms or officialese that obscure a plain meaning when a direct term exists (e.g., slum vs. depressed socioeconomic area). Allowed in technical incident reports or specialized documents where the technical term is standard or required. Example violation: impacted with the ground prematurely. NOT a violation: The accident report states the aircraft impacted terrain at 14:32Z.
- redundant_modifier: Adjectives/adverbs that merely restate information inherent in the base word or already clear from context (e.g., personal friend, smile happily). Keep modifiers that add a distinct nuance (e.g., smiled nervously). Example violation: a personal friend of mine. NOT a violation: She smiled nervously.
- unnecessary_appended_particle: A tacked-on particle (often “up”) that adds no meaning to the verb (e.g., order up → order). Keep particles that change meaning (pick up the kids; set up a tent). Example violation: order up. NOT a violation: We set up the tent before dark.
- empty_metadiscourse_lead_in: Self-referential throat‑clearing that announces the act of saying instead of saying it (e.g., It should be pointed out…). Acceptable only when the metacomment adds necessary scope or emphasis (e.g., To be clear, we excluded minors). Example violation: It should be pointed out .... NOT a violation: To be clear, we excluded children under five.
- timid_qualifier: Hedging intensifiers like a bit, sort of, in a sense, rather, quite, very, pretty used to soften a statement without a precise purpose. Allowed when signaling deliberate understatement, irony, or a specific degree that couldn’t be stated more exactly. Example violation: sort of. NOT a violation: It was almost impossible to hear.
- bureaucratic_preposition_stack: Three or more stacked prepositional phrases that bury action/agent and force rereading. Legal or standards language that must enumerate precise conditions is a reasonable exception. Example violation: Such preparations shall be made as will completely obscure all Federal buildings and non-Federal buildings occupied by the Federal government during an air raid for any period of time from visibility by reason of internal or external illumination.. NOT a violation: Fees are set in accordance with Section 12 of the code.

## verb_power_and_active_structure
Prefer people doing actions with strong, specific verbs. Avoid passives that hide agents, expletive subjects that bury meaning, and nominalizations that gum up the works.
- agentless_passive: Passive voice (be + past participle) with no agent where a clear agent exists and matters. Passive is fine when the agent is unknown/irrelevant or the receiver is the focus. Example violation: initiatives were undertaken. NOT a violation: My wallet was stolen.
- expletive_subject_opener: Starting sentences with There is/are/was/were or It is/was that postpone the real subject when a direct subject–verb rewrite would be clearer. Weather/time idioms (It’s raining) are fine. Example violation: There are various groups that think you can ban certain kinds of guns.. NOT a violation: It’s raining.
- nominalization_over_active_verb: Inflated abstract-noun constructions that replace a simple verb and blur agency (e.g., provide assistance → help). Acceptable if the concept itself is the topic (e.g., Inflation is rising). Example violation: provide assistance. NOT a violation: Inflation is rising.
- phrasal_verb_padding: Verb + preposition used where a single verb does the job without loss (e.g., set up a business → start/launch a business). Keep phrasals that are idiomatic or change meaning (set up a tent). Example violation: Don't set up a business that you can start or launch.. NOT a violation: We set up the tent at dusk.
- abstract_noun_as_subject_hides_people: An abstract noun (reaction, response, hostility, cynicism) as sentence subject with no people/agents present. Acceptable in general propositions where no human actor is relevant. Example violation: The common reaction is incredulous laughter.. NOT a violation: Cynicism is a common theme in satire.

## freshness_not_cliche
Avoid prefab phrasing—journalese, hype, buzzwords, and contrived openers. Write what only you would write, with original words that fit the idea and moment.
- journalese_stock_cliches: Stock newsmagazine idioms and prefab action phrases that signal borrowed voice and lazy logic. Example violation: shouldered his way in only to be met by a .22-cal. pistol slug crashing into his face.. NOT a violation: “Sending shock waves,” he said—then added, “though the effect was modest.”
- buzzword_verbifications_and_fad_words: Trendy verbified nouns and corporatespeak that add no precision in general prose (e.g., prioritize, interface with, impact as a verb, de‑impact, platform as a verb). Acceptable in narrow technical contexts where the form is standard. Example violation: We target goals and we access facts.. NOT a violation: The driver can interface with USB devices in host mode.
- adjective_as_plural_noun: Using adjectives/honorifics as plural nouns (e.g., greats, notables) instead of concrete nouns. Example violation: greats. NOT a violation: ‘The Greats of Jazz’ (as a proper title).
- hype_modifiers_shopworn: Shopworn promo words (e.g., famed, upcoming, staffers) that date prose and add no content. In tight UI labels or listings they may be tolerated for brevity, not style. Example violation: upcoming. NOT a violation: Upcoming events (calendar UI label).
- cliche_lead_tropes: Tired opening formulas Zinsser flags: the future archaeologist, visitor from Mars, one day not long ago, have‑in‑common quiz. Example violation: When some future archaeologist stumbles on the remains of our civilization, what will he make of the jukebox?. NOT a violation: In a lesson on clichés, you may quote them to critique the form.
- overblown_hyperbole: Outlandish comparisons and catastrophe metaphors that strain credibility or scale. Example violation: The living room looked as if an atomic bomb had gone off there.. NOT a violation: “It was a disaster,” she joked, rolling her eyes. (Clear as intentional overstatement in quoted speech.)

## momentum_leads_endings
Hook early, add concrete detail fast, and keep pressure building. Avoid meta-previews and schoolroom wrap‑ups; end with control and lift, not a dutiful summary.
- schoolroom_wrap_up_phrases: Formulaic ending openers that announce a summary (In conclusion, In sum, Therefore, as we have seen, To summarize) instead of delivering a crafted close. Acceptable in formal summaries specifically labeled as such. Example violation: In sum, it can be noted that.... NOT a violation: Executive summary: In conclusion, the board should... (labeled summary).
- filler_transition_crutches: Empty transition crutches that add no content (as stated earlier, overall, by the way, incidentally, a quick detour) and slacken forward pull. Permissible when pointing to a specific prior section or citation. Example violation: as stated earlier. NOT a violation: As noted in Section 3.2 of the 2022 report, …
- previewing_next_section_meta: Announcing structure instead of executing it (e.g., In the next section I will…). Acceptable in textbooks or formal outlines where previews are conventional and helpful. Example violation: In the next section I will.... NOT a violation: Chapter overview: In the next section we will derive…
- breakfast_to_bed_opening: Starting narrative with irrelevant routine (alarm clock, breakfast) before the action starts. Example violation: [A fishing trip begins with] the ringing of an alarm clock before daylight.. NOT a violation: An essay about insomnia may start with the 4:17 a.m. alarm.
- limp_paragraph_final_summaries: Paragraph‑final sentences that merely summarize (In short…, Therefore…) or trail off in hedges, instead of spring‑boarding the reader to the next idea. Occasional use to cap a dense technical explainer can be acceptable. Example violation: In short, the results were mixed.. NOT a violation: In short, the algorithm converges in O(n log n). (Caps a technical derivation.)

## anti_llm_slop
Penalize the recognizable habits of LLM-generated prose. These are not grammatical errors — the text is fluent — but tells that mark writing as machine-generated and voiceless. This pillar exists because plain English that sounds like a chatbot is still a failure.
- stock_transition: Formulaic connective or throat-clearing phrase: 'moreover', 'furthermore', 'it's important to note', 'it's worth noting', 'that said', 'in today's world', 'in conclusion', 'overall,'. Example violation: Moreover, it's important to note that budgets vary.. NOT a violation: Budgets vary.
- not_x_but_y: The contrastive reframe 'not just X, but Y' / 'not only X but also Y' / 'less about X, more about Y' used as emphasis filler. Example violation: This is not just a tool, but a whole new way of working.. NOT a violation: The tool changes how the team works.
- tricolon_habit: Reflexive rule-of-three listing ('clear, concise, and compelling') where the triple adds rhythm but no information. Flag only when the three items are near-synonyms or padding. Example violation: The plan is simple, straightforward, and easy to follow.. NOT a violation: He packed shirts, boots, and a week of canned food.
- summarizing_closer: A final sentence that restates what the passage just said ('Ultimately, ...', 'In the end, what matters is ...') or zooms out to an empty universal. Example violation: Ultimately, success comes down to preparation and persistence..
- empty_intensifier: Hollow emphasis characteristic of model prose: 'truly', 'deeply', 'incredibly', 'seamlessly', 'effortlessly', 'crucially', 'delve into', 'landscape', 'tapestry', 'journey' (figurative), 'game-changer', 'robust' (non-technical). Example violation: This truly transformative approach seamlessly delves into the data landscape..
- uniform_rhythm: A run of 3+ consecutive sentences with near-identical length and structure (subject-verb-object, no variation), producing the flat metronome cadence of generated text. Cite the run. Example violation: The team built the feature. The users liked the design. The metrics showed the impact. The company saw the value..
- hedge_stack: Multiple hedges stacked in one claim ('could potentially', 'may help to', 'can often be seen as'), signaling the model's reflexive liability-dodging rather than honest uncertainty. Example violation: This approach could potentially help to improve outcomes in some cases.. NOT a violation: This approach sometimes improves outcomes.
